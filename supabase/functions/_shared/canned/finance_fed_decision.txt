Fed holds rates at 3.75%, signals two cuts likely in second half of 2026

Washington — The Federal Reserve held its benchmark interest rate steady at a target range of 3.75% to 4.00% on Wednesday, the third consecutive meeting without a change, while updated projections from the Federal Open Market Committee signaled a majority of policymakers now expect two quarter-point cuts before year-end.

The decision was unanimous. Chair Jerome Powell, in a press conference following the announcement, said the committee was "appropriately patient" while waiting for clearer signals on whether the recent reacceleration in services inflation reflects a temporary supply shock or a more entrenched trend.

The Summary of Economic Projections, released alongside the rate decision, shifted the median dot for end-2026 to 3.375%, down from 3.625% in the December projection. Twelve of nineteen FOMC participants now favor at least two cuts, up from eight in December. Three participants project no cuts at all this year.

Powell emphasized that the projection is "not a plan" and that the committee remains data-dependent. "If services inflation remains above our objective in the coming prints, we are absolutely prepared to hold longer," he said. "If the labor market deteriorates faster than expected, we are equally prepared to move sooner."

Markets responded to the dovish shift in the dot plot. The two-year Treasury yield fell 11 basis points to 3.92%; the ten-year fell 6 basis points to 4.21%. The dollar index was down 0.4%. Equity markets rallied late in the session, with the S&P 500 closing up 0.9% at a fresh record high. Fed funds futures now imply a 68% probability of a cut by the July meeting, up from 41% before the decision.

The Fed's preferred inflation gauge, core PCE, ran at 2.7% year-over-year in the most recent print, above the 2.0% target but below the 3.1% reading from a year prior. Services inflation, which Powell flagged as "the area requiring the most patience," was 4.1% year-over-year, essentially unchanged from three months ago.

Labor market data has shown gradual cooling. The unemployment rate ticked up to 4.3% in the latest jobs report, the highest since November 2022. Payroll growth averaged 142,000 per month in the most recent three-month window, down from 196,000 in the prior three-month window.

Several economists who had projected a cut at this meeting revised their calls following the decision. Goldman Sachs moved its first-cut projection from May to July; JPMorgan moved from June to September; Bank of America held its existing September call.

The next FOMC meeting is May 6-7. The committee will receive two more CPI prints and one more PCE print before then.

Trump administration officials, who have publicly pressured the Fed for cuts, did not immediately respond to the decision. Treasury Secretary Bessent posted on social media that "the data has been telling the Fed what to do for months" but did not directly criticize the hold.
